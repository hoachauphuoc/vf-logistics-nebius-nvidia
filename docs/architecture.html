    03 · CHAIN OF CUSTODY

    Every hop narrows what the next one may do

    A finding from a model is a claim until something deterministic agrees with it. These are the eight places where that is enforced in code, in the order a case meets them.

    The document is the attacker

    Model Armor screens every upload in 400-character windows with 120 of overlap, so one planted line in a four-page bill of lading is not diluted below the threshold.

    injection_detected is never waivable

    A transcription fills fields, never verdicts

    untrusted.py whitelists what a document may set. risk_score, decision and state from a document are refused.

    forbidden_field is never waivable

    Two numbers never come from paper

    The route's average cost and the shipper's history decide whether a price looks wrong, so they come from our records. A document that supplied its own baseline could clear itself.

    baselines are ours

    The floor is one-way

    reconcile() takes the higher of model and floor; validate() raises if the HS or zero-day findings ever lowered it. A wrong release ships contraband; a wrong hold costs ten minutes.

    effective = max(model, floor)

    Authority is published, not earned

    A named person publishes a versioned Delegation Boundary. With none active, every protected action is refused, and a refused action turns the whole outcome into a proposal.

    no boundary ⇒ nothing runs

    A guest can look, not release

    The one-click judge session can block or ask for papers. Releasing cargo needs a password sign-in, and a release over open evidence needs the operator role, a reason and every waived item named.

    approve() over open evidence ⇒ 403

    Claim before send

    An approval is claimed with an optimistic lock before anything is called, so two clicks cannot both publish. The claim is a lease: a crashed holder's case is offered again after it runs out.

    one key per approval: exec-<approval_id>

    Applied once, downstream

    The receiver claims the key and applies the decision in one Firestore transaction, so a repeat is only counted. It accepts only a push Google signed for the configured account.

    no token ⇒ 403, nothing written
